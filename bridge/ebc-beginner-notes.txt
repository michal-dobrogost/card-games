Suits: ♠ ♡ ♢ ♣

Playing Hand
------------
Playing NT we count winners.
Playing Trumps we count losers from long-trump hand.

Signals hierarchy (1 ranks highest):
1) attitude (applies on first round of a suit led by partner)
   high => like, low => dislike
2) count (applies on (a) opponent leads a suit (b) second round of a suit led by partner)
   high, low => even, low high => odd
3) lavinthal (only if attitude and count don't apply: (a) discarding (b) giving partner a ruff (c) when suit is dead (eg. last card in dummy)
   high => lead higher ranking suit, low => lead lower ranking suit (ignore suit in play and trump suit)

Play high from the short side first (eg. Declarer AKTxx Dummy Qxx, play x from declarer and win with Q first).
- This unblocks the suit and allows us to keep playing in that suit (vs. forcing us to renter long side with a side entry).
- This provides an opportunity to ruff.

Lead through strength (ie. prefer stronger opponent in 2nd hand), up to weakness (weaker opponent in 4th hand).

Lead weakness towards strength (764 across KQ3 start with 4).

Finesse is any play that takes advantage of us playing after opponent.

Sequence of equal cards (QJTxx):
- lead the top of equal cards (Q): promotes the other cards, tells partner you may have lower cards (JT).
- play the bottom of equal cards (T): tells partner you may have higher cards (QJ).

If partner knows you have a card (eg. you opened with Q in QJ10x) and you only have J10x left in your hand, play the 10, it preserves knowledge for your partner (you still have the J).

When finessing lead high. Your 10 may be equivalent to QJ in partner allowing him to play low allowing you to take the hand and lead another finesse again.

Draw trump unless there's a reason not to.

Never lead away from the ace: Axxx play x as it let's opponent cash K.

When partner leads 4th card play high to promote their high cards.

Keep cards in opponents long suits to beat small cards in late tricks.

NT: Can delay taking taking tricks to create a void in opponent 1 which prevents entering opponent 2's hand with long suit.

Fin: Finesse
Eq: high from short side (with equivalent cards lead from from short hand first)
W2S: lead from weakness towards strength

West | East | Method | Play
---------------------------------------------------
AKx  | Qx   | Eq     | cash Q, low to K, cash A
KQx  | xxx  | Fin    | low to Q, bus to east, low to K (A in south)
AQJ  | xxx  | Fin    | low to Q/A, bus to east, low to J/A (A in south)
Axx  | KJx  | Fin    | K to x/A, bus to east, J to x/A
QJT  | Axx  | W2S    | Q to x/A, J to x/A, T to x/A


Bidding
-------
HCP (high card points): A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1
LP (length points): 5-card-suit=1, 6-card-suit=2, ...
DP (dummy / shortness points): void=5, singleton=3, doubleton=1
TP (total points): HCP + LP (opening new suit), HCP + DP (raising partner's suit)

25 points for game in NT or Major suit
29 points (+Ace) for game in Minor suit

Voids in hand:
- NT: voids are bad - lost lead can mean many more lost tricks in void suit.
- Trump: voids are good - opportunity to ruff.

NT counts HCP only

It becomes risky to bid after opponents bid 1NT because they have so much strength.

Bidding: Opening
----------------
Pass: 0-13 TP (with exceptions)

1NT: 15-17 HCP, balanced hand
Balanced hand: no void, no singleton, at most 1 doubleton

1 level suit bid: 13-21 TP
a) longest 5+ card major, higher ranking on tie.
b) otherwise longest 3+ card minor, higher ranking on tie (except 3-3 bid 1C).
   - Opening 1C: 16% of time will have 3 clubs.
   - Opening 1D: you will almost always have 4+ diamonds.

Preempts (weak 2 bids: 2S,2H,2D): Not enough TP to open @ 1 level, 6 card suit with 2/3 or 3/5 top honours.
- Typically 5-9/10 HCP

2NT: 20-21 HCP, balanced hand

2C (Artificial, forcing): 22+ TP or 9+ tricks in hand
- Want to get to game with <6 TP in partner
Artificial: says nothing about clubs


Bidding: Responding to 1-level major bids
-----------------------------------------
Opening  5 cards  6 cards  7+ cards
1♠ / 1♡  70%      25%      5%

Pass: 0-5 TP (with exceptions) => 6+ TP must make a bid.

Raising partner's major suit opening:
a) 2S/2H (raise): 6-9 TP, 3+ trumps
b) 3S/3H (jump-raise): 10-11 TP, 3+ trumps
c) 4S/4H: Weak freak: 5+ trumps, singleton or void, 0-7 HCP
   - Takes away bidding room from opponents
   - Our failed contract likely to score better than allowing opponent's to find contract.
   - Our weakness is likely our opponents strength (HCP, short suit).
d) XY -> 4S/4H: 12-15 TP (Bid another best suit first (forcing), then go to game)
   - Can't go straight to game contract (1 -> 4) because it means weak freak.
   - Bidding one short of game is not forcing so that doesn't work.
   - Bidding another suit gives bidding room for finding slam if possible and are forcing.

Not raising partner's major suit opening:
a) New Suit (forcing):
   @ 1 level: 4+ cards, 6+ TP
   @ 2 level: 4+ cards, 10+ TP (2H promises 5+ cards)
b) 3NT (signoff): 13-15 HCP, doubleton in partner's suit (no fit), balanced hand
c) 2NT (invitational): 11-12 HCP, doubleton in partner's suit (no fit), balanced hand
   - Hoping partner has a little extra and can bid 3NT
d) 1NT (invitational): a,b,c don't apply; 6-10 HCP, may not be balanced

Bidding: Responding to 1-level minor bids
-----------------------------------------
Opening  3 cards  4 cards  5 cards  6 cards  7+ cards
1♢       5%       40%      40%      15%      5%
1♣       15%      25%      35%      15%      5%

Pass: 0-5 TP (with exceptions) => 6+ TP must make a bid.

Assuming partner bid 1♢ try to find a major fit:
(a) 1♠/1♡: 6+ TP, 4+ cards in suit
(b) 1♢ response to 1♣: 12+ TP, 4+ ♢ and longer than major suits (partner will then bid 4+ card major if they have one)
All other bids deny 4 card major suit
(c) 2♢ (raise): 6-9 TP, 4+ diamonds or 5+ clubs, preferably shaped hand
(d) 3♢ (jump-raise, invitational): 10-11 TP, 4+ diamonds or 5+ clubs, preferably shaped hand
(e) 3NT: 13-15 HCP, balanced hand
(f) 2NT: 11-12 HCP, balanced hand
(g) 1NT: 6-10 HCP, balanced hand
(h) 2♣ response to 1♢: 10+ TP, 4+ cards

Bidding: Responding to 1NT
--------------------------
Distribution of opening with 1NT bid: 4333 20%   4432 50%   5332 30%

(a) 2♠/2♡/2♢ (signoff): 0-7 TP, 5+ card suit
    "better play with trumps given my bad hand"
(b) 3♠/3♡ (invite): 10+ TP, exactly 5 card major
(c) 4♠/4♡ (signoff): 10+ TP, 6+ card major
(d) 2♣ (stayman convention): 8+ TP, exactly 4 cards in a major
    "What are your 4+ card majors" response (2♠/2♡ - indicates major, 2♢ - no 4+ card major)
When none of the above apply (thus ~ balanced hand):
(d) pass(signoff): 0-7 TP
(e) 2NT (invite): 8-9 TP (good means have some length)
(f) 3NT (signoff): 10-17 TP

Opening Lead
------------
Against Suit Contracts, best leads:
(1) AK: lead A, then potentially K depending on what you see in dummy etc
(2a) Top of 2 honor sequence (K from KQxx)
(2b) Singleton (x from x): you will get a ruff immediately
(2c) Suit bid by partner
Additional leads:
(3) 4th best from a long suit with at least one honor (but not bare A)
(4) Top of doubleton (Q from Qx): this is a weaker lead
Bad leads (find a lead in a different suit instead):
- lead A no K: confuses partner expecting you to have K, sets up oponnents K/Q.
- lead in suit with bare ace (Axxx lead x) in suit contract: allows opponents to cash K.
- lead unsupported honors (Kxx lead K)

Against NT contracts, best leads:
(1a) Top of honor sequence (Q from QJTxx): promotes and develops through length
(1b) Suit bid by partner (Q from ♠Qx if partner bid ♠)
(2) 4th best from long suit with at least one honor (5 from ♠KJ753 if ♠ is longest): hopefully partner has honor which promotes your honors.

Giving up trick in led suit is OK in NT but avoid in suit contract.
With ♠ AQ962 ♡ xx ♢	KQJ ♣ xxx:
  against ♡ contract lead ♢K: avoid loosing trick.
  against NT lead 4th best ♠6: declarer may have stopper which will set up your ♠s.


Defending
---------
NT: primary objective is to setup cards in a long suit.

NT: Try to knock out attacker's high cards in a suit to take all card in suit later.
