Glossary
--------
Counting Top-winners:
- Sequence of highest cards across declarer and dummy (A,K,Q,...).
- Capped by the longer suit count between the two hands.
- Top-winner count is calculated across all suits.

Entry - A top-winner that allows us to transfer play into a hand.

Blocked - When winning high cards are stuck in one hand because other hand can't be reached.

Stopper - A top-winner that allow us to win back the lead.

Ruffing - winning a trick with a trump

Over-ruff - when one ruff is with a higher trump beating another ruff

Void - hand with zero cards of a suit
Singleton - hand with one card of a suit
Doubleton - hand with two cards of a suit

Drawing trumps - playing trumps to drain opponent of ruffing opportunities

Honours - A,K,Q,J,10

Sequence headed by honour - eg. 2 sequence: K,Q,X (where X is not an honour)

Singleton - a suit with a single card

Establishing a suit - playing until opponents run out of cards higher than yours in that suit.

Planning play
-------------
1) Count top-winners
2) Find extra tricks: promoting, establishing
3) What could go wrong?
4) Make plan

Promoting:
- A hand like K,Q,J does not have top-winners.
- Play these cards to "knock out" the A and promote these into winners.

Establishing a suit - Drawing out opponent's cards in a suit so they don't have any cards left.

Finessing:
- With A,Q in one hand lead in a lower card from the other hand first. If 2nd to play plays K, we play A, otherwise we play Q.
- This only works 50% of the time (when 2nd to play has K not 4th to play).

What can go wrong (NT):
- Stranded top-winners | to help: unblock, ensure entries, overtake
- Defenders establish long suite | to help: lose leads early so we can win them back.
- Don't notice a card is promoted | to help: count cards left and note high cards not yet played.

What can go wrong (Trump):
- Opponents ruff our top winners | draw trumps before cashing winners
- We run out of trumps to ruff | count ruff tricks needed and keep enough
- Opponents over-ruff | ruff with the highest card we can afford

Opening Leads
-------------
1) Set up tricks for our side
2) Not giving away tricks unnecessarily
3) Give information to partner

No Trump:
1) If have sequence of 3 headed by honour => lead top card
   [information to partner: have card below but not above]
2) Play from suit with most cards (if card count tied then most HCP) to establish suit
   a) have honour => play 4th highest card
      [low card may indicate 4 or more cards including honour]
   b) no honour => play 2nd highest
      [high non-honour card may indicate poor suit with no honour]

Suit Contract (w/ trumps):
1) If have sequence of 2 headed by honour => lead top card
2) Suit headed by honour (no ace!) => lead low (4th highest, or lowest from 3) card
3) A singleton (poor lead if partner has low HCP, best when we have low HCP increasing HCP in partner)
4) Doubleton => Highest card, next card at next opportunity indicates doubleton
5) Suit no honour => 2nd highest (MUD => middle, up, down)

Cardplay for defenders
-----------------------
second person to play in trick:
1) "2nd hand low" - low card led, play low (saves high cards for later trick in suit, partner still has opportunity to play high)
2) "cover honor with honor" - honour card led, beat with honour (promotes other cards, drains high cards from both opponnent hands)

third person to play in trick:
1) "third hand high" (wins or promotes)
2) "Lowest card doing job" (eg. sometimes inspecting dummy shows that we can play lower card and get same result)

Third, fourth to play in trick:
1) "lowest of equal cards" - play lower of equal cards [tells partner: we don't have card below]

Note partner's opening lead:
[honour card promises card below]
[low card (especially in NT) 4th highest in suit with honour]
1) take time to workout what it may mean about the missing hands
2) remember throughout play, further play helps us decide what it meant
If need to find a suit to lead in new trick, suit previously led by partner is often a good choice.



Split probabilities
-------------------
An odd number of cards is more likely to split evenly (eg. 3-2).
An even number of cards is more likely to split oddly (eg. 3-1).
